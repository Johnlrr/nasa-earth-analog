1. Read beside NASA's Artemis III requirements

"Surface slope for landing (100m): <10 deg" · "Surface slope limit for EVA (2km): <20 deg"
Artemis Human Landing System Overview, NASA 2025 International Space Apps Challenge (L. Ortiz, NASA, 4 Oct 2025), slides 21 and 23; same text in Artemis Human Landing System Update (A. Dwyer Cianciolo, NASA LaRC, 1 Aug 2024), slides 19 and 21 — NASA NTRS

With one formula on both worlds, 76 % of the Connecting Ridge footprint is steeper than 8°. In the 4 km footprints of 6 classic analog field sites the median is 9 %, a factor 8.6 lower; in the 12 km blocks around them, 0 of 54 footprints pass the terrain test for the ridge. About 10 km of traverse at Da Qaidam meets as much terrain steeper than 8° as 10 km on the ridge; at the median classic site it takes about 86 km; over the whole 12 km blocks, 12 km against 63 km (if the share along the path equals the areal share; a consistency check, since the engine selects on this share).

2. One Earth footprint for three south-pole sites of PGDA 78

The Da Qaidam cell is in the robust set of 3 of the 6 PGDA 78 south-pole sites (Connecting Ridge; de Gerlache rim and the peak near Shackleton are exploratory readings with a medoid reference) and stays there with NASA's NASADEM. It is a known Mars analog region: 71 analog papers in a Mars context, 1 in a Moon context. The Peary Land cell passes the Moon terrain test and the Mars ice test at one camp, but it is marginal: with a second DEM its rank in the strictest configuration is 19 where the rule needs 24.5.

3. What a field test can and cannot bring home

By this engine's measure a terrain site reproduces 1 of 7 test variables; 5 need a named facility and 1 is unobserved. No Earth site reproduces the polar Sun: the best one has the Sun below 7° for 28 % of daylight, the lunar target for 100 %.

Mars ice, read beside NASA's Mars Life Explorer concept study ("land within a vertical meter of Martian mid-latitude ice, drill for samples to a 2-meter depth"): of the 8 Earth cells the ice lens keeps, 2 have no modelled thaw; at the other 6 the modelled permafrost table is within the 2 m drill reach at 6 and within 1 m at 5. This is the top of permafrost from a thaw model, not an observed ice table; the ice lens is semi-quantitative.

4. Our own method, tested before we ran it

Registered in config/prereg_route_v1.yaml before any Earth footprint went through the planner. A slope-limited planner (grade limits 6°, 8°, 10°, 30 m cells, same seed on both worlds) measures how often points 1–2.5 km apart connect, how far routes detour and how much they climb. A footprint reproduces the ridge if it is as close as half of the ridge's own lunar neighbours.

The primary hypothesis fails (p = 0.15). Slope matches climb more than the ridge (40 vs 26 m per km); classic sites are flatter but half of their footprints fall inside the spread of the ridge's own neighbourhood. So a card now reports slope match and route match separately, and classic sites keep their value for route-planning rehearsal. Two open-country footprints outside known analog areas pass both on two DEMs: Argentina (-28.79, -69.63), the one random cell of 1,000 that passed, and Baffin Island (63.85, -65.58), where from 13 Nov to 22 Jan every daylight minute has the Sun below 7° (snow season; more terrain shadow than the ridge). This is a planner on 30 m cells, not a rover.

V2, registered before the rerun (config/prereg_v2_mars.yaml): V1 took its threshold from only 8 lunar neighbours. With 124 non-overlapping 4 km windows of the same lunar DEM, random land drops to 95 of 994, classic footprints to 17 of 54, and the same three slope matches pass: H1 PASS (p = 0.049), but classic sites are still not beaten (p = 0.59). A slope match is a better-than-random pointer to traverse-like terrain, not a better one than the classic sites.

4b. The same lens on Mars: Perseverance's Jezero delta front

Same registration. Target: a 4 km footprint of the Jezero western delta front on the USGS Mars 2020 CTX DTM (20 m, read at 30 m), 12 % of it steeper than 8°. Slope and route thresholds come from 185 windows of the same Mars DEM. Classic Mars analog footprints match in 17 of 45 cases (HI-SEAS 9/9, Atacama 4/9, Haughton 3/9, MDRS 1/9, Wright Valley 0/9), random bare land in 159 of 994: PASS (p = 0.0005). The delta front is gentle terrain, so matches are common (mostly hyper-arid plains in the Sahara and Arabia; 132 in open countries). None of the Moon-ridge slope matches matches Jezero: the two targets pick different terrain.

5. A campaign, not a site

Two leads together cover 5 of 5 field rows that orbit can judge; Baffin covers 4 at once, inside its winter window; the best classic site alone covers 1. Rocks under 60 m need a survey; 8 rows go to named facilities. Coverage across sites is a union, not a joint test environment (exploratory, Deviations 35).

6. Known truth

  • On the Moon, footprints of the Shackleton inner wall pass their own region 4 of 4; footprints of the other sites, 13 of 45. Ridge, rim and massif sites are not told apart (12 of 28 same site, 81 of 225 other sites, base rate 110 of 302): the test recognises a terrain class, not a named place.
  • Ice lens (semi-quantitative): 2 of 2 polar-desert controls pass, one of them the calibration site; 2 of 2 wet-permafrost decoys are rejected.
  • Mineral lens: 3 of 4 pre-registered checks pass; the stop rule is FAIL, so no mineral analog is published.

Counts of matching cells come from a 307-cell sample screened towards Connecting Ridge, not from a census: about 201 bare-land cells (95 % CI 10– 1,093) would pass the terrain test for the ridge, before the ice filter and the visual review.
